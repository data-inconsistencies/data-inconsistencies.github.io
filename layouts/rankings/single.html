New to ELO ratings?

An ELO rating is one number that sums up how strong a player is, based on who they have beaten. It was created by physicist Arpad Elo for chess and is now used in many games and sports.

  • Every player starts at 1500.
  • Beating a higher-rated player earns more points than beating a lower-rated one, and losing to a lower-rated player costs more.
  • Points move from loser to winner, so the total never changes.
  • The gap predicts results: 200 points ahead means you are expected to win about 76% of the time; 400 points ahead, about 91%.

How we use it: the players are analytics engines and each public benchmark report is a game. The report's finishing order (fastest first) decides who beat whom; a report that compares six engines counts as every pair of them playing at once. Reports are applied oldest first, so a rating reflects every benchmark an engine has appeared in, not just the latest one. Products of different kinds play in separate leagues ("playgrounds"): query engines are rated only against engines, and lakehouse table formats only against table formats. A report that compares both counts once in each league. Engines get faster, so older reports count for less: a report's weight halves for every two years it predates the newest one. Use the workload buttons to see ratings from one benchmark type only.

A rating measures speed in published benchmarks, many of them run by vendors. It says nothing about price, features or ease of use. More detail: Elo rating system (Wikipedia), a plain-language guide from Chess.com, and our exact formula below.

Method & contributing

Each N-way match is split into every pair of engines. For each pair the expected score is 1 / (1 + 10(Rb−Ra)/400); a win scores 1, a tie 0.5. An engine's change is w × K / (N−1) × Σ(actual − expected) with K = 32, so ratings stay zero-sum. The match weight w = 0.5age / 2 years, where age is measured back from the newest report in the data, so a report two years older than the newest counts half as much (shown as “weight” in the match log). The full spec is in docs/elo/spec.md.

The ClickBench column is for contrast: ClickBench is a single-table, scan-heavy benchmark, while most ELO matches are join-heavy TPC suites. It shows the product's best entry in ClickBench's default view: its rank among {{ hugo.Data.elo.clickbench.systems }} systems and its Combined score (×1.00 is the best system; lower is better). "—" means the product has no entry there; hover for why. Updated {{ hugo.Data.elo.clickbench.updated }}.
